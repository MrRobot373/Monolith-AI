# Strategy, plans and OKRs

## OKRs

- **Objective**: qualitative, inspiring, time-bound ("Make onboarding effortless for new SMB customers this quarter").
- **Key results** (2–4 per objective): measurable outcomes, not tasks, with baseline → target.
  - Good: "Median time from sign-up to first invoice: 3 days → 1 day"
  - Bad: "Launch onboarding wizard" (that's an initiative)
- **Initiatives**: the projects expected to move the KRs, each with an owner.
- 3–5 objectives max per team per quarter. Score at quarter end 0.0–1.0; 0.7 is a good stretch result.

```markdown
## O1: Make onboarding effortless for new SMB customers (Q4 2026)
| KR | Baseline | Target | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median sign-up → first invoice | 3 days | 1 day | Maya |
| Onboarding support tickets per 100 sign-ups | 22 | 10 | Lena |
Initiatives: guided setup (Raj), import from Excel (Raj), welcome email series (Sam)
```

## Business plan (SMB)

Executive summary · Problem and customers · Offer and pricing · Market and competition (see
`market-research`) · Go-to-market (channels, sales process) · Operations · Team · Financial plan
(revenue model, costs, break-even, cash needs; see `financial-analysis`) · Risks · Milestones.

## Strategy work

1. Where are we (facts, metrics, SWOT)? 2. Where do we want to be (vision, 1–3 year goals)?
3. Where will we play and how will we win (customers, offer, differentiation)? 4. What will we not do?
5. Initiatives, owners, budget, sequencing. 6. How we'll measure and review (cadence).

Be concrete: name customer segments, numbers, dates. Separate assumptions from facts and propose how
to test the riskiest assumptions cheaply.

## Done when

Goals are measurable with baselines, initiatives link to goals with owners, trade-offs and
"not doing" are explicit, and the plan is in a format the team can track.

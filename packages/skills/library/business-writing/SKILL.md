# Business writing

Readers skim. Put what they need first, make the ask unmistakable, and cut everything else.

## Before writing (answer in your head)

- Who reads it, what do they already know, how do they feel about the topic?
- What should they know, decide or do after reading? By when?
- What's the one sentence that matters most? That goes first.

## Structure (BLUF: bottom line up front)

1. **Opening**: the point or the ask in 1–2 sentences.
2. **Context**: only what's needed to act or agree.
3. **Details**: bullets, a short table, or numbered steps.
4. **Close**: the next step with owner and date; who to contact.

## Emails

```
Subject: Approval needed by Fri: renew design tool licences ($2,400/yr)

Hi Maya,

Could you approve renewing our 8 design tool licences by Friday 10 Oct? They expire on the 15th.

- Cost: $2,400/year (same as last year)
- Alternative: drop to 6 seats and save $600; Ravi and Lena would lose access
- I recommend renewing all 8; both are on the Q4 campaign

Reply "approved" and I'll handle the rest.

Thanks,
Sam
```

- Subject = topic + action/deadline. One email = one topic.
- Specific dates ("Friday 10 Oct"), amounts, names, links.
- For several asks, number them so replies can reference them.

## Tone

| Situation | Approach |
|---|---|
| Bad news | State it plainly early, give the reason, what you're doing, what they can do. No over-apologizing. |
| Complaint reply | Acknowledge the specific problem, take ownership of your part, give the fix and timeline. |
| Saying no | Reason in one line, offer an alternative if any. |
| Asking a busy senior person | Make it answerable in one line (yes/no or pick an option). |
| Announcement | What's changing, when, why, what people need to do. |

## Plain language rules

- Short sentences (15–20 words avg), active voice, everyday words ("use", not "utilize").
- Cut filler: "I just wanted to reach out to", "please do not hesitate to", "at this point in time".
- Specific beats vague: "by Thursday 3 pm", not "soon"; "12% fewer errors", not "significantly better".
- No jargon or internal acronyms for outside readers; define them once if needed.
- Match the organization's style guide or brand voice skill if one exists.

## Formats

- **Memo / internal update**: Title · TL;DR (3 bullets) · Details · Decisions needed · Next steps.
- **Formal letter**: sender block, date, recipient, subject line, salutation, body, closing, name/title.
- **Policy**: Purpose · Scope · Policy (numbered rules) · Responsibilities · Exceptions · Effective date.

## Deliver

Give the final text ready to copy (and as a file if asked: `.md`, or `.docx` via `word-documents`).
If the email should be sent through a connected mail app, draft it there; sending needs the person's
approval. Offer one alternative only when tone is a judgment call (e.g. firmer vs softer).

## Checklist

- [ ] The first two lines carry the point/ask.
- [ ] Every name, date, number and link is correct.
- [ ] Tone fits the reader and situation.
- [ ] Could anything be deleted without loss? Delete it.

## Done when

The message is ready to send, the reader knows exactly what to do, and facts are verified.

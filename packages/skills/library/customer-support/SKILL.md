# Customer support

Solve the customer's problem in as few back-and-forths as possible, and leave them feeling heard.

## Replying to a customer

1. **Understand**: what happened, what they want (fix, refund, answer, to be heard), how upset they
   are, account/order details. Check the knowledge base, order data or connected tools before
   answering; never invent policies or promises.
2. **Structure**:
   - Acknowledge the specific issue (not "we apologize for any inconvenience").
   - Answer or fix, with exact steps or what you've done.
   - Next step and timeline if anything is pending; who will follow up.
   - Close warmly and briefly.
3. **Tone**: human, calm, plain words; mirror their formality; no blame; no jargon; one apology is
   enough.

```
Hi Daniel,

Sorry your order arrived with a cracked lid — that's not the experience we want you to have.

I've sent a replacement today (tracking: 1Z999AA10123456784, arriving Thursday). No need to return
the damaged one.

If the replacement has any issue, just reply here and I'll sort it out.

Best,
Mira · Support
```

## Difficult situations

| Situation | Approach |
|---|---|
| Angry customer | Acknowledge feelings and facts, own what went wrong, concrete fix, avoid defensiveness |
| Refund outside policy | Explain the policy briefly, offer what you can (partial, credit), escalate if warranted |
| Bug report | Thank, reproduce steps, workaround, set expectations (no fixed dates unless known), log it |
| Can't help | Say so clearly, explain why, point to who can |
| Public review | Short, polite, no private data; move details to a private channel |

Refunds, credits, account changes and anything legal need a human's approval in most companies:
draft the action and flag it.

## Help-center articles

Title as the customer's question ("How do I change my billing address?"), one-sentence answer,
numbered steps with exact button names, screenshots placeholders, troubleshooting, related articles.
Write for scanning; one task per article.

## Macros (canned responses)

Keep a library (`macros.md`): name, when to use, text with placeholders (`{{first_name}}`,
`{{order_id}}`), and internal notes. Personalize before sending.

## Ticket triage and summaries

For a batch of tickets: categorize (billing, bug, how-to, feature request, complaint), priority
(urgent: outage/payment failure/security), sentiment, and suggested owner. Summarize trends with
counts and examples; save as CSV/Excel for the team.

## Done when

Replies answer the actual question with correct facts and a clear next step, tone fits, any
actions needing approval are flagged, and articles/macros are reusable.

# Market research

Research is only useful if the reader can trust it: cite sources, separate facts from estimates, and
say how confident you are.

## Gather

Use `web_search` (several targeted queries) and `web_fetch` on primary pages: competitors' websites
and pricing pages, annual reports, government statistics, industry associations, reputable news.
Follow the `web-research` skill for source quality. Note date and URL of everything you use.

## Competitor analysis

Comparison table (one row per competitor, including the person's own company):

| Company | Target customer | Core offer | Pricing (as published, date) | Strengths | Weaknesses | Positioning / message | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then: where the market is crowded, gaps/unmet needs, how the person's company can differentiate
(2–4 concrete options). Pricing must be quoted exactly with the date seen; mark "not published".

## Market sizing

- **Top-down**: total market from a credible report × the relevant share.
- **Bottom-up** (preferred): number of potential customers × adoption rate × annual price.
  ```
  SMB accounting firms in Maharashtra: ~12,000 (source)
  × fit for our product 40% = 4,800 (SAM)
  × reachable in 3 years 5% = 240 customers × ₹60,000/yr = ₹1.44 Cr (SOM)
  ```
- Show every input with its source or mark it as an assumption; give a range, not false precision.

## Segments and personas

Segment by needs and behavior (not only demographics). Persona: role, goals, pains, current
alternatives, buying triggers, objections, where they get information. Base on evidence (reviews,
forums, interviews); label invented details as hypotheses to validate.

## SWOT

Strengths/weaknesses are internal (vs competitors); opportunities/threats are external (market,
regulation, technology). Each bullet specific and evidenced; end with "so what" implications.

## Trends

For each trend: what's changing, evidence (data point + source), timeframe, implication for the
person's business.

## Deliver

`market-research.md` (or Word/PDF): executive summary with key takeaways first, then sections above,
then numbered sources. Spreadsheet of the comparison table if useful.

## Done when

Claims have sources with dates, estimates show their inputs and ranges, the comparison is fair and
current, and the summary gives clear implications.
